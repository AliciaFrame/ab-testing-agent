What this comparison does—and does not—show

Controlled: identical development case/run keys, frozen Decision-1 router, same baseline specialist prompts and tool boundaries for MAI and GPT-6 Sol, four-worker initial baseline evaluations, max output 32,768 tokens, same tool-step limit and canonical grader. Actual provider-returned model names and policy hashes are checked before this report builds.

Optimization: task-specific prompt proposals from GPT-5.2, plus diagnostic variants derived from optimizer-split failures, are scored with the canonical grader. One diagnostic variant checks incident-window membership arithmetically through the existing calculate tool; no statistical tool or grader is changed. Search uses separate MAI capacity and an 8,192-token output limit for both its baseline and candidates. Selected prompts are checked at the matched 32,768-token limit on all 45 development episodes for that task. Changed specialists are rerun; unchanged specialists reuse their frozen baseline measurements, identified in the evidence below. Development data is used for selection, so optimized results are selection-set performance—not an unbiased final-test estimate.

Validity candidate development was prefetched on isolated MAI capacity at two workers, overlapping baseline transport recovery. Candidate records retain their inference endpoint. Other changed-task development checks use the primary deployment. This further limits causal interpretation of latency differences; accuracy uses the same model version, prompts, tools, output limit and canonical checks.

Round 4: hypothesis-driven candidates from development failure analysis (experiments/round4.json): numeric checks via calculate for the 0.001 traffic-split threshold, incident window, Holm and power inputs; batched tool calls for portfolio; and per-specialist low reasoning effort. GPT-5.2 also proposed one candidate where search failures existed. Candidates needed no fewer optimizer-split passes than their search baseline (or, when that baseline already passed every search case, went straight to development; this rule was added mid-round after attribution saturated), then were checked on all 45 development episodes for that task. A task change was kept for an accuracy gain, or for no accuracy loss with at least 10% fewer tokens. Ship decision and attribution ran on the isolated MAI deployment at two workers; other tasks on the primary deployment. Azure Resource Manager confirms both deployments serve MAI-Code-1.1-Flash version 2026-09-15; the isolated one reports its deployment name (abtest-mai-flash) as the returned model. Round-4 measurements reuse round-3 episodes for unchanged tasks. Selecting on development twice compounds selection bias; the final split remains sealed.

Round-4 portfolio override: batched portfolio tool calls cut portfolio tokens 40% for one extra failed episode (84.4% vs 86.7%). The pre-set round-4 rule rejected it; the user explicitly accepted the trade. It is recorded as an override in the optimization manifest and stays within the documented per-task limits (drop at most 5 points, at most 3 stable cases lost).

Round 5: targeted fixes from a review of round-4 development failures (experiments/round5.json): a scientific-notation rule for the 0.001 threshold in planning, the proven incident date check for ship decision, and a list-valued sample-sufficiency check for portfolio, all at default reasoning effort. GPT-6 Sol replaced GPT-5.2 as the prompt proposer, seeing optimizer-split failures only. Same search gate and development acceptance as round 4.

Sol on final prompts: to keep the setup identical, GPT-6 Sol was rerun with the exact final specialist instructions and settings for every task whose prompt or settings changed. Unchanged tasks reuse Sol's baseline-prompt episodes. Policy hashes and runtime contracts are asserted equal between optimized MAI and this Sol configuration.

Operational latency: MAI and Sol have different deployment capacities. These are observed timings under the available service conditions, not capacity-normalized claims about intrinsic model speed. Total tokens describe the final benchmark episodes; SDK retries within an attempt are included in its latency. Discarded transport attempts are accounted for separately above. Search/generation and pilot traffic are separate experimental overhead. Unchanged-task retry overhead is reused with its baseline measurement.

Final split: sealed and not used for model selection or this comparison. No scenario labels or expected answers enter the router or specialist during execution.
